# Pool Pump Priming — Recovering from Lost Prime

Reference for re-priming a single-speed pool pump (strainer pot style) after it has lost prime.

Typical causes of lost prime:
- **Low water level:** skimmer sucks air
- **Pump run dry:** e.g. after draining, backwashing, or a closed valve
- **Air leak on suction side:** lid, lid O-ring, unions, valve glands
- **Pot opened for service:** basket cleaning, motor or seal work

Values below are from the Pentair WhisperFlo owner's manual (model WFET-6 / 011528).

## Quick Reference

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Max run time to achieve prime (manufacturer) | 8 minutes |
| Practical stop time if pot is not swirling | 1–2 minutes |
| Water fill level in pot | Up to the bottom of the inlet port |
| Lid tightening | By hand only, no wrenches |
| Pump speed during priming | High (single-speed pumps: n/a) |

## Before You Start

**Pool water must be above the drain and skimmer line closed.** If the level is too low, the pump will pull air and never prime. Top up first.

**Do not open a hot or pressurized pot.** If the pump has been running dry or failed to prime, turn off the pump, open the filter air release valve, and let it cool before touching the lid.

## Priming Procedure

1. Turn the pump off at the switch
2. Raise the pool water level
3. Open the filter air release valve and stand clear of the filter
4. Remove the lid clamp and lid (turn counterclockwise by hand)
5. Fill the strainer pot with water, up to the inlet port
6. Check the lid O-ring is clean, greased, and seated
7. Reinstall the lid and clamp, hand tight
8. Turn the pump on
9. Watch the clear lid — water should swirl in the pot within about a minute
10. Close the air release valve once a steady stream of water (no air) comes out

**If the pot is not swirling after 1–2 minutes:** turn the pump off, and diagnose before trying again. Running dry damages the shaft seal.

## Stuck Lid

A lid that will not turn after a dry run or failed prime is usually holding trapped pressure or vacuum.

1. Turn the pump off and let it cool
2. Open the filter air release valve and let the air bleed off (can be slow, several minutes)
3. Try the lid again by hand
4. If still stuck, crack the strainer pot drain plugs a turn or two (bucket underneath) to break the seal
5. Do not pry or use a wrench on the lid — it can crack

Water gushing out when the lid comes off is a good sign: the pot and suction line are full.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Will not prime, bubbles in pot | Air leak | Check lid, lid O-ring, suction unions, suction valve glands |
| Will not prime, pot not filling | Not enough water | Refill pot and volute; check pool level; confirm suction valves are open |
| Weak flow after priming | Clogged strainer or skimmer basket | Clean baskets |
| Air never stops from filter valve (pump running) | Suction-side air leak | Inspect lid gasket and suction plumbing |
| Drips between motor and pump body after a dry run | Damaged shaft seal | Replace shaft seal |

## Notes

- Self-priming pumps can start with an empty pot if the system is airtight, but a dry run after draining or service should be treated as needing a fill
- A cracked, warped, or poorly seated lid is a common cause of repeated prime loss
- Check for leaking between the motor and pump body after any dry run — seal damage shows up afterward
- If the pump will not prime after repeated attempts with a full pot and correct water level, stop and inspect for suction leaks or an impeller blockage
