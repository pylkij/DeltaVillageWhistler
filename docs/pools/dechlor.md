# Pool Chlorine — DeChlor Dosing

Reference for lowering free chlorine using Ecolab Aqua Balance Dechlor (sodium thiosulfate).

Pool volumes:
- **Pool:** 143,800 L
- **Indoor:** 6,600 L
- **Outdoor Large:** 6,100 L
- **Outdoor Small:** 3,200 L

Values below show the amount of DeChlor needed to lower free chlorine by the stated number of ppm in each pool.

## Formula

**1 g of DeChlor per 1,000 litres lowers free chlorine by 1 ppm**

Amount (grams) = Desired ppm reduction × (Pool volume in litres ÷ 1,000)

Reduction needed = Current chlorine reading − Target chlorine reading

## Product Rate

1 lb of Aqua Balance Dechlor lowers chlorine by approximately 12 ppm in a 10,000 gallon pool (Ecolab recommended rate - aligns with expected chemistry). Converted to metric, this works out to just under 1 g per 1,000 L per ppm (≈0.9986 g), rounded to 1 g for the formula above.

## Worked Example

**Pool:** 143,800 L
**Current chlorine:** 10 ppm
**Target chlorine:** 3 ppm
**Reduction needed:** 7 ppm

Dose:
7 × (143,800 ÷ 1,000) = 1,006.6 g ≈ **1 kg**

**Procedure:**
1. Weigh out ~1 kg DeChlor
2. Pre-dissolve in water (bucket) and add with the pump running
3. Circulate the pool
4. Retest chlorine after 30 minutes
5. Add more only if the reading is still above target, based on the actual result

## Approximate Volume

Dry DeChlor is roughly **1 g per mL** by bulk density, so the gram amounts above are about the same number of mL. This varies with granule size and how tightly it packs — weigh it when accuracy matters.

Example: 46 g (Indoor, 7 ppm reduction) ≈ 46 mL

## Notes

- Active ingredient is sodium thiosulfate (CAS 7772-98-7). Product is Ecolab Aqua Balance Dechlor, item 6117422 (4.5 kg pail)
- Do not overshoot — too much DeChlor strips all chlorine from the water, leaving the pool unsanitized
- Always retest before adding a second dose
- Sodium thiosulfate works via a fast reduction-oxidation (redox) reaction that converts reactive chlorine into non-toxic chloride ions. Efficacy of this reaction is **not** impacted by pH, temperature, or TDS
