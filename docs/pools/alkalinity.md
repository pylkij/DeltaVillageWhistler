# Pool Alkalinity — Baking Soda Dosing

Reference for raising total alkalinity using sodium bicarbonate (baking soda).

Pool volumes:
- **Pool:** 143,800 L
- **Indoor:** 6,600 L
- **Outdoor Large:** 6,100 L
- **Outdoor Small:** 3,200 L

Values below show the amount of baking soda needed to bring each starting alkalinity reading up to 120 ppm (top of target range).

## Formula

**180 g of baking soda per 10,000 litres raises alkalinity by 10 ppm**

Amount (grams) = (Desired ppm increase ÷ 10) × 180 × (Pool volume in litres ÷ 10,000)

## Staged Dosing Rule

For corrections **larger than 30 ppm**, add only **75%** of the calculated amount first, then retest before adding the rest. This avoids overshooting on large corrections.

## Worked Example

**Pool:** 143,800 L
**Current alkalinity:** 40 ppm
**Target alkalinity:** 120 ppm
**Increase needed:** 80 ppm

Full dose:
(80 ÷ 10) × 180 × (143,800 ÷ 10,000) = 20,707.2 g ≈ **20.71 kg**

Since this is a >30 ppm correction, apply the 0.75 staging factor:
20.71 kg × 0.75 ≈ **15.53 kg**

**Procedure:**
1. Add ~15.53 kg baking soda
2. Circulate 4–6 hours (overnight preferred)
3. Retest alkalinity
4. Add remaining amount based on actual reading — do not assume it hit target exactly

## Notes

- Target range for most pools: 80–120 ppm total alkalinity
- Baking soda affects pH only mildly — use soda ash instead if the primary goal is raising pH
- Low starting alkalinity (like 40 ppm) often correlates with unstable pH — check pH at the same time
